Swords & Shields

  • Goal: get a sword pointing at the enemy head (the crowned cell) and schedule that reaction.
  • Turn: first create a ready piece on your creation grid — pick it from your box, then click one of the four rotation handles that appear around the grid. Creation is skipped automatically if the grid is occupied. Then each of your pieces on the board may act once.
  • A piece's eight options are laid out around it: the four faces (adjacent cells) move it one step; the four corners rotate it, each showing a preview. The top corner is the 360° turn — it just marks the piece done.
  • Anything in the path of a move is pushed along. Anything pushed off the board — including into a head or a void corner — dies.
  • Reactions, on every touching pair of sides, friend or foe: sword vs sword → both die; sword vs bare side → the bare piece dies; sword vs shield → the sword piece bounces back one cell; sword vs head → that head's owner loses.
  • Pending reactions appear as numbered markers. You schedule them one at a time — resolving one may cancel the others. The chain must be finished before you act again.
  • A move whose reactions never end is an illegal move: you will notice while scheduling it, and take it back with Undo or Restart turn.
  • Handicap: the first player begins with the blank piece — the weakest of the 24 — already sitting on their creation grid.
  • Computer opponent: pick who plays each side under New game. Easy weighs single actions. Medium and hard search whole turns — sequences of several actions — then play your best answer out against each candidate turn before choosing; hard examines twice as many turns and looks much deeper for your replies. All of them take a win they can see, and it refuses any line that leaves you a win in one action — unless every line does. Beating it means trapping it there. You can watch it weigh lines on the board while it thinks, and Undo pauses it so you can take a move back.
  • Online play: under New game pick Online play and agree a game name with your friend. Whoever types it first waits; the second to arrive starts the game, and sides are handed out automatically. A name already being played is refused, so pick another. Moves travel directly between the two browsers, so both of you must keep the page open.
  • Draw if every sword-bearing piece is dead. Keys: Esc cancels, U undoes.
